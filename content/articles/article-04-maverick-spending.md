## Every Unplanned Purchase Costs More Than the Item

A chef needs a case of premium olive oil for a function tomorrow. The regular supplier cannot deliver in time. The chef calls another supplier, gets it delivered, signs the delivery docket, and moves on.

The invoice arrives three weeks later. No purchase order. No contracted rate. No approval. The price is 40% above what the hotel pays through the approved supplier. By the time the AP team catches it, the function is long over and the chef is working on next month's menu.

This is maverick spending. And in hotels, it is everywhere.

## What Is Maverick Spending?

Maverick spending is any purchasing that happens outside the approved procurement process. It includes buying from unapproved suppliers, purchasing items not on the approved catalogue, creating purchase orders without proper authorization, or bypassing the system entirely and paying via expense claims or petty cash.

In hospitality, maverick spending is particularly difficult to control because:

- Multiple departments have purchasing authority (kitchen, housekeeping, maintenance, F&B, front office)
- Many purchases are urgent and operational, not planned in advance
- Suppliers call department heads directly and develop relationships that bypass the procurement process
- Staff turnover means retraining is constant
- The culture is service-first, which can frame procurement controls as obstacles rather than protections

None of these factors make maverick spending acceptable. They make it predictable. And predictable problems have systematic solutions.

## The Real Cost of Uncontrolled Purchasing

Most hotel finance teams focus on the price premium of maverick purchases. That is the visible cost. The actual cost is higher and runs across several categories.

**Price premium.** Off-contract purchases typically cost 15 to 40% more than the same items sourced through approved suppliers with negotiated rates. Over a year, across multiple departments and properties, this adds up to tens of thousands of dollars in preventable overspend.

**Compliance exposure.** Purchasing outside approved channels creates audit gaps. When an auditor asks for the paper trail on a specific expense, a purchase made directly by a department head with no PO and a verbal approval leaves the hotel exposed.

**Budget invisibility.** If a kitchen team spends $3,000 outside the approved system, that spend does not appear in the budget tracking until the invoice arrives. The finance team is making decisions based on incomplete data. By the time the overspend is visible, the month is over.

**Supplier fragmentation.** Maverick purchasing creates supplier relationships the procurement team does not know about. This makes renegotiating contracts harder, because you do not know the full scope of what you are buying from whom.

**Staff time.** Chasing invoices with no PO, reconciling unmatched transactions, and investigating discrepancies costs AP and finance team hours that should be spent on analysis, not detective work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What percentage of hotel purchasing is typically maverick spending?**
Industry benchmarks suggest that 20 to 30% of hotel purchasing happens outside approved procurement processes. For hospitality-specific operations, particularly in F&B, this figure can be higher due to the urgent, variable nature of kitchen purchasing.

**Can you eliminate maverick spending completely?**
No procurement system eliminates it entirely, and the goal should not be zero tolerance at the cost of operational paralysis. The goal is to make approved purchasing so easy that maverick spending becomes the exception rather than the default. PurchasePlus customers consistently reduce off-contract purchasing by more than 60% within the first year.

**Does controlling maverick spending slow down urgent purchasing?**
Not with PurchasePlus. The platform is designed for hospitality operations, where urgency is normal. Mobile PO creation, pre-approved supplier catalogues, and configurable approval thresholds mean urgent purchases can be authorized in under two minutes without bypassing the system.

**How does PurchasePlus handle purchases that genuinely need to happen outside the approved catalogue?**
The platform includes an emergency purchasing workflow with mandatory post-hoc documentation. The purchase is still captured in the system, still generates a PO, and still feeds into budget tracking. The difference is that the approval happens after delivery rather than before, with automatic escalation and audit flagging.
