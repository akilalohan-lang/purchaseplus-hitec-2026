## Most Hotels Use 4 to 7 Disconnected Systems for Purchasing

Ask a hotel finance director to describe how a purchase order becomes a paid invoice in their operation. The answer is almost always a story involving multiple systems, multiple handoffs, and at least one step where someone exports a spreadsheet to send to someone else.

A typical hotel purchasing cycle looks something like this: suppliers are contacted by email or phone. Purchase orders are created in a separate PO system, or on paper. Deliveries are signed off in the receiving area with a paper docket. Invoices arrive by email and are forwarded to accounts payable. AP staff manually match invoices to POs in the accounting system. Approvals happen via email chains. Payment runs are processed in the accounting system or a separate payment tool.

Each of these steps involves a different system, a different person, and a different data format. The information that should connect them does not transfer automatically. It is retyped, re-entered, or manually reconciled.

This is fragmented purchasing. And the cost of it is higher than most hotel operators realize.

## The Cost of Disconnection

Fragmented purchasing creates costs at every join in the process.

**Manual data entry errors.** Every time information is transferred from one system to another manually, error rates increase. A purchase order number mistyped when entering an invoice into the accounting system creates a matching failure that requires hours to investigate.

**Approval delays.** When purchase approvals happen by email and invoice approvals happen in a separate system, approval chains are invisible to the people waiting for them. Urgent purchasing decisions wait on email responses. Invoices queue in AP backlogs while approvers are chased.

**Duplicate payments.** Without automatic PO-to-invoice matching across a unified system, duplicate invoices are a persistent risk. Suppliers who know this exploit it. Duplicates that do get caught cost investigation time. Duplicates that do not get caught are a direct loss.

**Audit gaps.** A complete procurement audit trail requires matching every invoice back through approvals, delivery receipts, and original purchase orders. Across disconnected systems, this reconstruction is a manual project. For multi-property groups, it can take weeks.

**No spend intelligence.** Disconnected systems produce disconnected data. A hotel with a separate PO system, accounting system, and supplier management spreadsheet cannot easily see total spend by supplier, or cost per cover by outlet, or budget variance in real time. These reports require a manual compilation exercise that is always out of date by the time it is finished.

## What Procure-to-Pay Actually Means

Procure-to-pay is not a category of software. It is a description of a complete workflow: from the decision to procure something (the first step) through to the payment of the supplier (the last step), with every action in between connected in a single system.

A complete procure-to-pay platform covers:

- Approved supplier catalogue management
- Purchase order creation and approval workflows
- Purchase order transmission to suppliers
- Delivery receipt confirmation (three-way matching)
- Invoice ingestion and automated matching
- Exception management and approval routing
- Budget commitment tracking
- Payment processing and supplier remittance

When all of these steps happen in one system, the data flows automatically. A purchase order created in the morning is matched to the invoice that arrives in the afternoon without manual intervention. The budget reflects the commitment from the moment the PO is approved. The audit trail is complete and instantaneous.

## One Platform vs Many Point Solutions

The argument for best-of-breed point solutions (a specialist PO tool, a specialist invoice processing tool, a specialist supplier management tool) is that each does its specific job better than an integrated platform can.

In some technology markets, this is true. In hotel procurement, the value of integration outweighs the marginal capability difference between a specialist point solution and a purpose-built platform.

The reason is that hospitality procurement is a connected workflow. The value is not in any single step. It is in the connection between steps. Real-time budget commitment only works if PO creation and budget tracking are integrated. Straight-through invoice processing only works if invoice matching and PO data are in the same system. Supplier performance analytics only work if all purchasing activity runs through a single supplier record.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is procure-to-pay in hospitality?**
Procure-to-pay in hospitality refers to the complete procurement cycle from purchase requisition through to supplier payment, managed within a single integrated platform. It includes supplier management, PO creation, delivery confirmation, invoice processing, and payment, connected so that data flows automatically between each stage.

**How many systems does the average hotel use for purchasing?**
Industry benchmarks suggest that mid-size hotel groups use between 4 and 7 systems for purchasing-related functions, including PO software, accounting systems, supplier portals, spreadsheet tracking, email approvals, and payment tools. Each connection between these systems creates manual work and error exposure.

**What is three-way matching in hotel purchasing?**
Three-way matching is the process of confirming that the purchase order, delivery receipt, and supplier invoice all agree before approving payment. PurchasePlus automates three-way matching as part of the procure-to-pay workflow, flagging discrepancies automatically rather than requiring manual reconciliation.
