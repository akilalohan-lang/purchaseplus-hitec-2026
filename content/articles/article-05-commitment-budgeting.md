## Month-End Surprises Are a Management Failure, Not a Finance Problem

Every hotel finance director has been in this meeting. The month closes. The numbers come in. The F&B cost percentage is higher than budgeted. The executive team asks why. The finance team explains that a $4,000 catering order placed three weeks ago was not on the budget. The kitchen director says they thought it was approved. The operations director says no one told them.

The invoice arrived after the fact. The budget was tracking green. The reality was red.

This is not a people problem. It is a data problem. And it is entirely preventable.

## How Traditional Hotel Budgeting Works (and Where It Breaks)

Traditional hotel budgeting operates on invoiced actuals. The finance team sees costs when invoices are processed and posted to the accounting system. Purchase orders, if they exist, often live in a separate system that does not feed the budget reporting in real time.

The result is a budget that tells you what was spent last month, not what has been committed this month. By the time the finance team can see a problem, the money has already been spent.

For F&B specifically, this lag creates chronic budget pressure. Food and beverage purchasing is frequent, variable, and split across multiple cost centres. A hotel's kitchen team might place 50 to 100 orders per week from 20 or more suppliers. If none of those orders are reflected in the budget until the invoices arrive (often 30 to 60 days after the fact), the budget is always looking backwards.

## What Commitment-Based Budgeting Means

Commitment-based budgeting means the budget reflects every authorized spending commitment, not just invoiced spend.

When a chef raises a purchase order for $800 worth of produce, that $800 is immediately committed against the F&B cost budget, even though the invoice will not arrive for days. When a maintenance team approves a repair quote, the amount is committed against the maintenance budget before the work is done.

The finance team sees the real financial position of every cost centre at any moment, not the historical position from last month's invoices.

## Seeing Live GP% Before the Invoice Arrives

The most operationally useful output of commitment-based budgeting in hospitality is live gross profit percentage.

In hotel F&B, GP% is the core financial metric. It tells the operation whether their cost of goods sold is within target relative to revenue. Traditional systems calculate GP% at month-end or week-end, using invoiced food costs against revenue that has already been recorded.

PurchasePlus calculates live GP% using committed spend rather than invoiced spend. When a function menu is confirmed and the corresponding purchasing is ordered, the system shows the projected GP% for that function before any invoices have arrived. If the number is off, the kitchen team can adjust the purchasing before the costs are locked in.

This is not a forecasting tool. It is a real-time operational signal that lets the people managing cost also control cost.

## The Practical Impact for Multi-Property Finance Teams

For a finance director overseeing multiple properties, commitment-based budgeting changes the nature of the monthly review.

Instead of reviewing last month's costs and investigating variances that have already been incurred, the team reviews current commitments across all properties and intervenes on trends before they become variances. A property that is running 3% above its F&B cost target in committed spend is visible mid-month, when there is still time to adjust purchasing, renegotiate an order, or flag an approval workflow that is too permissive.

The monthly close becomes a confirmation exercise rather than a discovery exercise. The variances that exist are small, explainable, and already in the process of being addressed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is commitment-based budgeting?**
Commitment-based budgeting means the budget includes every authorized purchasing commitment, not just invoiced actuals. A purchase order approved today appears in the budget today, before the goods are delivered and before the invoice arrives.

**How is this different from traditional PO-based budgeting?**
Traditional PO-based budgeting often captures commitments but in a separate system that is not integrated with real-time budget reporting. PurchasePlus integrates PO creation, budget tracking, and financial reporting so the commitment is visible immediately in the live budget.

**What is live GP% in hotel F&B?**
Live gross profit percentage is the projected F&B gross profit based on committed purchasing costs and confirmed revenue for a given period, calculated in real time rather than at month-end. It gives kitchen and F&B managers an operational signal, not just a historical report.

**Does this require changing the accounting system?**
No. PurchasePlus integrates with major hotel accounting and PMS systems and provides commitment data as a layer above the accounting system. Month-end actuals still feed into the accounting system as normal. The commitment visibility is an operational tool that sits alongside it.
